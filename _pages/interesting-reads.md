- Oct 2026: [Computer Chips Are the Most Magnificent Things Humanity Has Ever Made (The New Yorker)](/interesting-reads/Computer_Chips_Are_the_Most_Magnificent_Things_Humanity_Has_Ever_Made.pdf)
  <br>Gideon Lewis-Kraus argues that the semiconductor supply chain, not the moon landing, is modern civilization's great collective monument. A long look at how chips are made, how manufacturing left the United States, and why they now sit at the centre of geopolitical rivalry.
- Oct 2026: [How the D.S.A. Learned to Love Elections (The New Yorker)](/interesting-reads/How_the_DSA_Learned_to_Love_Elections.pdf)
  <br>Benjamin Wallace-Wells reports on the Democratic Socialists of America, a group of about 130,000 members that believes in class revolution and is nonetheless winning primaries. Follows the Los Angeles chapter's internal fight over endorsing a mayoral candidate.
- Jul 2014: [Of Microchips and Men: A Conversation About Intel (The New Yorker)](/interesting-reads/Of_Microchips_and_Men_A_Conversation_About_Intel.pdf)
  <br>A 2014 interview with Michael S. Malone, author of "The Intel Trinity," on Intel's origins under Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore, and how the integrated circuit and the microprocessor shaped science, finance and culture.
- Oct 2026: [The Danger of Getting Too Close to a Source (The New Yorker)](/interesting-reads/The_Danger_of_Getting_Too_Close_to_a_Source.pdf)
  <br>Bob Woodward recounts his decades-long friendship with Richard Darman, a White House insider who was also his source. It ended when Woodward's 1992 articles on the Bush White House exposed what Darman had told him. A reflection on where friendship and reporting collide.
- Oct 2026: [The First Social Network (The New Yorker)](/interesting-reads/The_First_Social_Network.pdf)
  <br>In rural Patagonia, isolated shepherds and gauchos still send each other personal messages over a daily AM radio program. A portrait of a community held together by a technology most of the world has abandoned.
- Aug 2026: [The Children a Scientist Took Home (The New Yorker)](/interesting-reads/The_Children_a_Scientist_Took_Home.pdf)
  <br>Carleton Gajdusek won a Nobel Prize for his work on a disease in Papua New Guinea, but his biggest experiment was on the children he brought back to the U.S. A look at the ethics and human cost of his life's work.
- Feb 2023: [ChatGPT Is a Blurry JPEG of the Web (The New Yorker)](/interesting-reads/ChatGPT_Is_a_Blurry_JPEG_of_the_Web.pdf)
  <br>Ted Chiang likens large language models to lossy compression: they return paraphrases of the web rather than exact quotes. He argues this shapes what we should expect from them, and whether we'd prefer them to search.
- Apr 2020: [Baking Bread in Lyon (The New Yorker)](/interesting-reads/Baking_Bread_in_Lyon.pdf)
  <br>Bill Buford, a newcomer to Lyon, apprentices at a neighborhood boulangerie. He finds that learning to bake bread reveals a whole way of life.
